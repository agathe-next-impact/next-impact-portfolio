The brand style guide is the normative document that governs a brand's entire visual identity. It formalizes the rules for using the logo, colors, typography and imagery, ensuring communication consistency across all media, whether print or digital.

## Why is a brand style guide important?

The brand style guide ensures consistency across all of an organization's communication materials. It serves three main functions:

- Strengthen brand recognition through the repetition of consistent visual elements.
- Formalize positioning and values through coherent graphic choices.
- Ensure uniformity of production, regardless of the contributor (in-house team, freelancer, agency).

A structured brand style guide is both an operational tool for production teams and a strategic lever for brand positioning.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Why invest in a brand style guide?">
A formalized brand style guide reduces back-and-forth with providers, ensures immediate consistency across all media and accelerates the onboarding of new collaborators or agencies. The time invested in its creation pays off from the first productions.
</Callout>

## The steps to create your brand style guide

<StepByStep>
<Step number={1} title="Define brand identity">

Before tackling graphic choices, formalize the brand's fundamentals. These strategic elements will guide all visual decisions:

- **Values**: the principles that structure the brand. For example: innovation, sustainability, technical expertise.
- **Mission**: the reason the company exists and the value it brings to its clients.
- **Vision**: the long-term strategic objective.

These three elements form the reference framework that ensures alignment between brand identity and its graphic expressions.

</Step>
<Step number={2} title="Choose the color palette">

Colors influence the cognitive and emotional perception of the brand. They contribute to the visual association between the company and its sector.

**Method for defining the palette:**

- **Dominant color**: select a primary hue aligned with brand positioning. Blue is associated with trust and stability; red with energy and engagement.
- **Secondary colors**: add 2 to 3 complementary hues that enrich the palette without saturating it.
- **Contrast check**: control contrast ratios (WCAG AA standard: minimum ratio of 4.5:1 for body text) to ensure legibility on all media.

Recommended tools: [Coolors](https://coolors.co/) or [Adobe Color](https://color.adobe.com/fr/).

</Step>
<Step number={3} title="Select typefaces">

Typography contributes directly to brand perception and content legibility.

- **Heading font**: a typeface with strong visual personality to structure information hierarchy.
- **Body font**: a typeface optimized for continuous reading, with clear rendering on screen and on paper.
- **Limit on fonts**: a maximum of two to three type families to maintain consistency.

Example: a brand with a technological and contemporary positioning will opt for a sans-serif font (Inter, Lato, Raleway), while a brand with an institutional positioning will favor a serif font (Garamond, Baskerville, Libre Baskerville).

</Step>
<Step number={4} title="Design the logo and its variations">

The logo is the most visible element of the brand style guide. It must be distinctive, relevant and adaptable.

**Design criteria:**

- **Simplicity**: a clean logo is more memorable and more legible at small sizes.
- **Adaptability**: plan variations (monochrome, with or without baseline, horizontal and vertical versions) to meet the constraints of different media.
- **Relevance**: the logo must visually convey the brand's positioning and sector.

</Step>
<Step number={5} title="Define complementary visual elements">

Beyond the logo and colors, the guide must frame all graphic elements:

- **Imagery**: define a consistent photographic style (reportage, lifestyle, packshot) or illustrative style (flat design, isometric, line).
- **Iconography**: select an icon set with a uniform style (outline, filled, duotone) and document usage rules.
- **Shapes and patterns**: if the brand uses recurring graphic elements (geometric shapes, textures), formalize their application rules.

</Step>
<Step number={6} title="Draw on sector references">

Analyze the brand style guides of recognized brands in your sector or in adjacent sectors:

- **Google**: vivid primary color palette, airy layout, proprietary typography (Google Sans) -- consistent with an accessible and technological positioning.
- **Coca-Cola**: exclusive use of red, iconic script typography -- maximum consistency for over a century.

The aim of this analysis is to identify transferable best practices while asserting the singularity of your own identity.

</Step>
<Step number={7} title="Roll out and apply the brand style guide">

Creating the guide isn't enough; its rigorous application is what makes the difference.

- **Team training**: make sure all contributors (graphic designers, developers, marketing team) know and apply the rules defined.
- **Comprehensive documentation**: produce a visual guide detailing each element, its rules of use and examples of compliant and non-compliant applications.
- **Validation across media**: test the guide on the main media (website, social networks, print) to verify its adaptability and consistency.

</Step>
</StepByStep>

<HighlightBox title="The essential elements to document" accent="blue">

The brand style guide must cover the following elements:

- **Logo**: primary versions, variations, monochrome, exclusion zones and minimum sizes
- **Colors**: HEX, RGB, CMYK and Pantone codes for each color in the palette
- **Typography**: fonts for heading, subhead and body text, with allowed weights and sizes
- **Imagery**: photographic, illustrative and iconographic style
- **Usage rules**: spacing, prohibitions (forbidden uses) and application examples

</HighlightBox>

## Tools to produce the guide

Several tools allow you to design and formalize a brand style guide:

- **Canva Pro**: suited to teams without advanced graphic design expertise, with brand-guide templates ready to customize.
- **Adobe Illustrator**: the reference tool for professional vector design.
- **Figma**: a collaborative tool that allows simultaneous work and the management of shared components (design system).

<Callout variant="tip" title="Versioning the guide">
The brand style guide is an evolving document. Plan a periodic review (annual or semi-annual) to adapt it to brand evolutions and team feedback. Version the document (v1.0, v1.1, v2.0) to maintain a clear history of changes and make it easier to track them.
</Callout>

## Visual consistency, a credibility factor

A rigorous brand style guide is a structuring investment for brand positioning. It ensures visual consistency across all communication channels and strengthens the trust relationship with users. By following this methodical process and using the right tools, you have what's needed to formalize a professional and lasting visual identity.
